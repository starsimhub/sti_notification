Act 1 · treated ≠ cured  ·  real model result

Even the women we do cure are reinfected within the year.

We traced 100 women cured of chlamydia for 12 months. Nearly half were reinfected — and almost always by their own untreated regular partners, not by sex-work contacts.

The bottleneck isn't a hidden reservoir PN can't reach. It's the woman's own concurrent partners going untreated — which partner notification can address.

Act 1 · the collateral harm

Syndromic diagnosis also drives unnecessary partner notification.

Most women presenting with vaginal discharge actually have bacterial vaginosis, not an STI — but syndromic management treats them as STI cases and prompts them to notify partners. Each notification carries a risk of intimate-partner violence.

A test that says "this is BV, not an STI" prevents a notification that was never warranted — an ancillary benefit of accurate diagnosis we can quantify.

Act 2 · the question  ·  real model result

Can point-of-care diagnostics break this — and how?

Two levers, and they act on different parts of the problem:

Partner notification (treat her partners) cuts prevalence — but barely touches new infections.

Condoms / counselling for the diagnosed cut incidence — the new infections PN can't stop.

Used together they're synergistic: PN drags more partners into treatment, enlarging the pool condoms then protect. The combined corner reaches the lowest prevalence and incidence.
